The Problem: Bilateral Trap & The Rate Illusion
Why traditional supply-chain financing fails suppliers and capital providers alike
Slide 2 of 10
❌ Traditional Bilateral Model
  • Single-Lender Monopsony: Supplier approaches one bank; takes whatever rigid terms are offered.
  • Zero Price Discovery: Locked into 70% advance caps and 3-to-5 day turnaround times.
  • The "Lowest Rate" Fallacy: A 9% loan offering only 70% advance fails an MSME needing payroll funds today.
  • Information Asymmetry: Lenders have no real-time audit trail of invoice validity or single-buyer caps.
✅ TradeCapital Marketplace Solution
  • Continuous Competitive Auction: Banks, NBFCs, and Fintechs compete simultaneously.
  • Multi-Dimensional Matching: Optimizes advance %, speed, fees, and tenor — not just nominal APR.
  • Automated 3-Way Reconciliation: Programmatic verification against GST and NIC manifests.
  • Dynamic Liquidity Recycling: Instant repayment rebalancing and single-buyer portfolio caps.
Speaker Note: "A supplier needing ₹16 Lakhs on a ₹20 Lakh invoice cannot survive on a 9% bank offer that only advances ₹14 Lakhs after 3 days. They need ₹18 Lakhs in 2 hours even at 11.5% — and our math engine solves this."

Risk Scoring Formulation
Risk Score = 0.45·Buyer + 0.25·Supplier + 0.15·Tenor - 0.50·Uncertainty
  • Tier 1 (AAA/AA): Spread +0.0%, Max Advance 90%.
  • Tier 2 (A/BBB): Spread +0.8%, Max Advance 80%.
  • Tier 3 (High Risk): Spread +2.2%, Max Advance 65%.
Electronic 3-Way Fraud Quarantine
  • Checks active status on GST Portal.
  • Validates eWay bill against NIC manifest.
  • Reconciles PO, invoice amount, and buyer acknowledgement.
  • Score < 60/100: Instantly quarantined; capital providers protected.
Speaker Note: "We account for incomplete information. If an unverified supplier or missing PO arrives, our uncertainty penalty widens the risk spread and lowers the maximum allowable advance."

Dynamic Liquidity & Single-Buyer Portfolio Caps
Protecting institutional balance sheets while enabling continuous capital velocity
Slide 7 of 10
Single-Buyer Concentration Enforcement

Lenders set maximum exposure caps per enterprise debtor (e.g. ₹100L cap on Global Motors). When a large deal arrives, over-exposed lenders are excluded autonomously while unconstrained lenders compete.

Continuous Liquidity Recycling

Upon escrow settlement by the enterprise debtor, principal capital + earned interest yield returns immediately to the lender's liquid pool for subsequent redeployment.

Speaker Note: "Our platform actively protects lenders from single-debtor defaults through programmatic exposure caps, while maximizing capital velocity through dynamic recycling."
